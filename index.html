Investigation revealed

The risk lived between the controls.

The case evidence points to an indirect access path: a contractor could access a deployment workspace, the workspace could retrieve a secret, and the secret enabled a workload identity to invoke a production service with broader authority. The key weakness is the combined effective access—not necessarily one invalid permission.

The reconstructed access path

Follow the relationship chain that should have been evaluated as a whole.

Contractor account Workspace access
Not a direct admin
→
Deployment workspace Can retrieve a stored secret
→
Workload identity Secret enables service access
→
Production service Broader data permissions

Root cause to examine

Trust and permission relationships were reviewed separately instead of as one effective access path. A valid contractor permission and a valid workload identity permission combined to expose a capability that was not apparent from either profile alone.

What to validate next

Confirm actual secret-access controls, whether the contractor could use the workspace to obtain or invoke the secret, the workload's exact production scope, and whether logs correlate to this path. These are investigation steps, not assumptions beyond the evidence.

Architectural response

Restrict deployment-secret access, remove unnecessary paths from workspace users to production credentials, scope workload permissions to required actions and resources, and evaluate effective access across trust relationships.

Identity security lesson

Least privilege must be evaluated across the full chain. A user may have no direct admin role and still reach powerful capabilities through delegated access, secrets, service principals, or workload identities.
